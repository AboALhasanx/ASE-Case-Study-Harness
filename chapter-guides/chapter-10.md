## Case-study questions (only if approved)

1. Does the target project publicly claim a specific product, process, or component certification?
2. If a claim exists, who issued it, what exact scope/version/standard does it cover, and can a primary source verify it?
3. Which quality-assurance artifacts are visible (for example, verification records, problem reports, configuration records), and what remains unavailable?

## Evidence and method

- Search the pinned repository's official documentation, release notes, and project site for explicit certification statements; save exact URLs and access dates.
- Verify any claim against the certifier's primary records and the relevant standard's current official documentation. Record product/version and certification scope precisely.
- Keep certification distinct from a project's self-reported testing, quality badges, CI, security scans, or contributor qualifications.
- If no verifiable certification is found, report **No claim verified in the examined sources**; do not assert that the software is uncertified globally.

## Pitfalls and reviewer checklist

- Do not infer certification from compliance language, process maturity, or quality tooling.
- Do not apply the chapter's historical examples as current standards without checking official sources.
- Make the optional status explicit and get instructor approval before counting this chapter toward coverage.
- Check that the evidence identifies issuer, standard, scope, version, validity period, and source provenance; disclose unavailable details.
